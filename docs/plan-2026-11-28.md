## Phase A: Nothing unchecked reaches readers (S1–S2)

### S1. A gated data branch and safe Release files

**Staging branch:**
- Every writer pushes to a new `data-staging` branch: `nightly.yml`, `compare.yml`, `validators.yml`.
- `scripts/publish_data.sh` takes the branch as an argument; its retry and rebase logic stays.
- `scripts/promote_data.sh` fast-forwards `data` to the staging commit, only when that run's gate and Frictionless checks passed. It refuses anything that isn't a fast-forward.

**Every writer runs the gate:**
- `compare.yml` and `validators.yml` run `pipeline.invariants --light` before promoting.
- The three workflows share one concurrency group, `data-writer`, so promotions run in series.
- A new script bootstraps `data-staging` from `data`.

**Gate failure:**
- `data` stays at the last good commit.
- The watchdog's "Data check" issue names the failed rules.
- `scripts/anomaly.py` adds a tier-4 rule for "staging ahead of data for more than 26 hours".

**Gate report:**
- Each promotion writes `checks/gate.json` on `data`: rules run, problems (0), notes, the staging commit and when it ran.
- Pages shows "Every figure here passed N automatic checks on <date>" in the footer.
- The Limits page explains it.

**Release files:**
- New uploads get content-addressed names: `stop-events-<date>-<sha8>.parquet`, and the same for trip tracks, tables and raw. The manifest names the exact file.
- Superseded files are deleted only after promotion (`pipeline/release.py` `prune_superseded`). A failed run can never break a file that a published manifest points to.
- Readers that build names themselves read names from manifests or `files.csv`. Check `examples/`, `scripts/site_views.py` and `scripts/site_data.py`.
- Old fixed names stay valid. There's no rename of existing assets; new names apply from each file's next rebuild.

**Tests:**
- New test: promotion refuses a non-fast-forward.
- New test: a failing gate leaves `data` unchanged (a temporary git repo in pytest).
- New test: `release.upload` names files by content.
- I12 accepts both name forms.

**Verify:**
- Dispatch the nightly. `data` moves only after the gate step.
- Force a gate failure on a scratch branch with `workflow_dispatch` input `force_gate_fail` (test only): `data` doesn't move, and the issue opens.
- Run `compare.yml` and `validators.yml` once each.

**Docs:** README and the data-branch README say the branch only ever holds checked figures, and point to `checks/gate.json`.

## Phase B: Numbers that say how sure they are (S3–S5)

### S3. Close the first-stop and last-stop gap; relays; deadhead (`events/0.6.0`)

Measured November 27: last stops timed about 75%, against roughly 89% at middle stops.

**Last-stop arrival:**
- When reports stop short of the terminal, accept arrival evidence from three sources:
  - the next trip of the same vehicle starting at that terminal (continuity, `pipeline/events/continuity.py`)
  - a layover dwell inside 40 m (about 130 feet)
  - a stop-sequence change to the last stop, which the feed reports
- New `report_source` values name each source.

**First-stop departure (`past_when_first_seen`):**
- Use the vehicle's previous trip end and its deadhead approach, carried in from `continuity`.

**Relays:** two vehicles on one trip (a driver swap brought out by van). Join the track pieces when vehicle A's reports end and vehicle B's begin within 5 minutes and 300 m (about 1,000 feet) on the same shape. Count relays in `continuity.relays`.

**Deadhead split:** in `quality` over-coverage, split reports between trips into "deadhead" (moving between terminals or to and from the depot) and "unassigned".
- Depot zone: the stretch between Greenbank and Coupeville, from Davin's fact.
- Version: `quality/0.8.0`.

**`by_route_stop` gains `position`** (first, middle or last), `ontime/0.6.0`, so the site and the gate can show the gap by position.

**Tests:**
- synthetic terminal ending, relay and deadhead cases in `tests/helpers/synthetic.py`
- golden update
- I9 and I10 extended to relays

**Measure:** replay November 19 to the latest day. Report observed share by position, before and after. Target: last stops within 5 points of middle stops. Watch false positives with check 2 (agreement with the feed's stop sequence).

### S4. The honest headline (`periods/0.3.0`)

**Untimed reasons, pooled:**
- Pool `not_observed_by_reason` over the window into `headline.json` → `current.untimed`: counts per reason in plain names.
  - `gap`: the feed went quiet
  - `skipped`: the bus left out that part of the route
  - `not_reached`: the bus stopped reporting before it got there
  - `no_reports`: the trip sent nothing
  - `past_when_first_seen`: the bus was already past when it first reported
- Plus a split by stop position from S3.
- I8 reconciles these counts with the day files.

**Worst-case range:** `current.bounds`, low = on_time / scheduled and high = (on_time + untimed) / scheduled. These are Manski's no-assumption bounds.

**Adjusted estimate:** `current.adjusted`, a nonresponse-weighted on-time share.
- Each timed departure is weighted by 1 / (share timed in its route × hour band × stop position cell). Cells with under 10 timed departures are merged.
- The interval comes from the same trip bootstrap (reuse `trip_interval` in `pipeline/periods.py`, `SEED`). Show it only when the weights stay under 10.
- I8 recomputes it from `by_trip` plus cell counts kept in `headline.json`.

**Site, kept light** (Neon's point), on Home:
- the big number with one line, "of 12,602 departures we could time, out of 14,664 scheduled"
- the split bar and the three stats
- one compact "How sure are we?" fold below the stats, holding:
  - the chance interval
  - the adjusted estimate
  - the worst-case range
  - a reasons bar ("why 2,062 departures weren't timed")
  - a link to Limits
- The schedule-change note moves into the fold, unless a change happened in the last 7 days.

**Elsewhere:**
- The Data quality page gets a "Why departures weren't timed" chart over time (the period switch).
- The Limits page explains all three ranges in plain words.
- Methods gets new subsection 4.x, "How sure we are", with the formulas and citations.

**Tests:**
- Fixtures: `quality/daily.csv` gains `untimed_*` columns (`pipeline/columns.py`). Rebuild the site fixture.
- Unit tests for the bounds and the weights, including a zero-cell case.
- A browser test for the fold.

## Risks

- **S1 changes how every writer publishes.** Keep `data` untouched until the first promotion passes; roll back by pointing writers at `data` again.
- **Content-addressed Release names:** check that every reader goes through manifests first (`examples/`, `site_views.py`, `pipeline/check.py`).

- **Free-plan limits:** Pages 1 GB, Supabase 500 MB and 5 GB egress, Actions minutes (`reproduce.yml` stays small: 3 days a week).
