2. The site runs on its own, with automated and agent checks.
3. Radical simplification and clear understanding.
4. Interactive visuals, with raw data always downloadable.
5. Fair comparisons with other bus systems.

Three audits, three code maps, a printed-timetable check (64 Firecrawl credits) and a design pass found the following.

## Phase 1: Accuracy (sessions 1–4)

### 1A. Publishing gate, version lock, effect report (no figure changes)
- **New `pipeline/invariants.py`** (`python -m pipeline.invariants --data <dir> [--releases <dir>] [--light]`). It reuses `pipeline/stats.share`, `pipeline/common.load_json` and `DayFiles`, and `pipeline/events/routes.window_of`/`pool`.
  - I1: `recent.json` overall equals the sum of the day files in its window.
  - I2: in each day file, total = Σ`by_route` = Σ`by_hour` = Σ`by_route_stop`, for scheduled, observed, on_time, early and late.
  - I3: `on_time + early + late = observed` and `observed ≤ scheduled`. Every share recomputes from its counts.
  - I4: rows of `ontime/daily.csv` and `quality/daily.csv` equal their day JSON.
  - I5: window rules, with holidays excluded.
  - I6: every day carries the current stage versions; a backlog under the 7-day cap is only a note.
  - I7: every Pacific day since the start has raw, parsed, events and quality entries, and quality `events` is not null on a service day.
  - I8: period files reconcile (Phase 3).
  - I9: every `stop_times` row has a stop event, and statuses come from the known set.
  - I10: vehicle chronology. On one vehicle, trip P's last arrival is no later than trip N's first departure plus 30 s. Deviations are within ±2 h.
  - I11: a nightly recount of the last 3 days from the SHA-checked stop-events parquet with `ontime.classify` equals the day files.
  - I12: the existing `pipeline.check`.
- **`nightly.yml`:** move `pipeline.check` and the invariants step above "Redeploy the site". The dispatch runs only if both pass. **`pages.yml`:** run `--light` before `scripts/site_data.py`.
- **New `scripts/check_versions.py` + `pipeline/versions.lock.json`.** It stores an AST hash per stage of the modules that compute its numbers. Comments and docstrings are ignored. Example: events = `infer.py`, `geometry.py`, `gtfs/schedule.py`, `geo.py`, `stats.py`. CI fails if a hash changes while the version does not.
- **New `scripts/effect_report.py`.** It reports headline figures before and after, observed share by first, mid and last stop and by status, and per-route on-time change. Its output is pasted into `docs/decisions.md` for every change to figures.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_invariants.py` (each invariant fails on a corrupted copy of the golden output) and `tests/test_versions_lock.py`.
- **Accept:** green on every published day. An edited `recent.json` in a rehearsal blocks the dispatch.

### 1B. Direction-aware timepoints (`timepoints/0.2.0` → events and quality rebuild)
- `pipeline/gtfs/timepoints.py` matches each printed name only among stops that the Passio trips of that route **and direction** actually serve. Timepoints are keyed by (route, direction, stop). `pipeline/events/__main__.py` and `ontime.summarize` use that key.
- **Printed-time check:** commit `reference/timetable-times-2026-09-27.csv`. It is parsed from the saved guide text with a reviewed parser, `scripts/parse_timetable.py`, and the PDF is not read again. Each version records `printed_time_agreement_share` and its largest gap. Known guide typos go into an overrides file with notes.
- **Tests:** a failing case where the opposite-direction stop is chosen today (route 60 NB Bayview), and a fixture with one stop 3 minutes off.
- **Accept:** the 10 known wrong-side cases resolve to the stops the buses actually use. `effect_report` shows timepoint coverage rising, not falling.

### 1C. Glitch filter (`events/0.4.0`, `quality/0.6.0`)
- **New shared `pipeline/glitch.py`.** It works on each vehicle's timeline, deduplicated and sorted by time, whatever the trip id.
  - **Spike:** report *i* is removed when the speed into it and the speed out of it both exceed 130 km/h (about 80 mph), but i−1 to i+1 does not.
  - **Plateau:** a run of 120 s or less, bounded by an impossible jump out and one back, whose neighbors agree with each other, is removed.
  - **Not removed:** a one-way jump. It is counted as `unresolved_jumps`.
  - **Cross-vehicle:** a removed report that sits within 30 m and ±10 s of another bus is also counted on its own.
- **Where it applies:** `infer_day` drops flagged reports before `track()`; new trip-tracks column `glitch_reports`. In `quality/metrics.measurement`, the old count stays and new counts are added.
- **Test fixture:** add a glitch run to `tests/helpers/synthetic.py` `RUNS`, then `pytest tests/test_golden.py --update-golden` and review.
- **Accept:** on 2026-11-24, about 60–67 reports are removed and the changes are confined to those buses. Other days move less than 0.5% of events. Check 2 is no worse.

### 1D. Follow the physical bus (`events/0.5.0`)
- **New `pipeline/events/continuity.py`,** called from `infer_day`.
  - Pass 1 works as today and builds each vehicle's ordered list of trips.
  - Pass 2, for each trip T on vehicle V:
    - **Attach late:** prepend V's reports from max(T.start − 30 min, the previous trip's last arrival) up to T's own first report, under any trip id, null included. `track()`'s `START_ZONE_M` keeps depot driving out.
    - **Switch early:** append V's reports after T's last own report until T.end + 30 min, `end_at`, or V passes the departure boundary of its next trip.
    - **Layover:** one report may time both P's arrival and N's departure; I10 enforces the order.
    - **Depot legs:** reports before the first trip and after the last are labeled `deadhead`. `over_coverage` splits them out.
    - **Driver swap / relay:** bridge null-id and other-id gaps on V. Join two vehicles one after another (overlap ≤ 60 s, continuing along the shape) as a relay. True duplicates keep the max-count rule and are counted.
    - **`block_id`:** check whether Passio has it. If so, use it only as a check (`block_disagreements`), because the feed is the truth.
- **New columns:** `report_source` on stop events (own / borrowed_before / borrowed_after / relay); `reports_borrowed_before`, `reports_borrowed_after` and `relay_vehicles` on trip tracks.
- **Docs:** methods §3 rewritten in plain prose, with the depot and route-change facts.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_continuity.py`, plus `RUNS` extended for six cases: id switch before the last stop, id carried past the next trip's first stop, null-id gap, relay, deadhead along the shape, real duplicate.
- **Accept, on the 2026-11-25 replay:**
  - last-stop `not_reached` 170 → 60 or fewer;
  - first-stop `past_when_first_seen` 92 → 30 or fewer;
  - mid-route not-observed does not rise;
  - the route 60 field-check error is no worse;
  - check 2 stays at 95% or above;
  - I10 passes.

## Phase 2: Runs on its own (sessions 5–6)
- **New `scripts/anomaly.py`** (standard library only, so `tests/test_light_imports.py` passes), called by `scripts/watchdog.py --data`.
  - **Baseline:** for each day type (`gaps.day_type`), the median and MAD of the last 28 service days, with a MAD floor. Fixed thresholds apply under 5 days of history.
  - **Tier 1, problem** (exit 1, fails the run): an invariant fails; stale versions; a service day with no events; zero scheduled trips; trip-id match below 0.95; coverage z < −4 or a drop > 0.15; a day unprocessed after 36 h; a workflow `disabled_inactivity`; the token expires within 7 days.
  - **Tier 2, look** (exit 1, comment on "Data check"): impossible jumps z > 4 and more than 20 (2026-11-24 would trigger); position age p95 more than 2× baseline; off-shape z > 3; observed or terminal observed z < −3; on-time |z| > 4; trip-offset flag; printed-time gap > 120 s; feed agreement < 0.9; token expires within 21 days.
  - **Tier 3, info** (exit 3, no failure): new Passio version, canceled trips, holiday list ending. Written to `quality/notes.json` for the site.
  - `watchdog.yml` is adjusted to these exit codes.
- **New `scripts/ping.sh start|success|fail`,** called by `nightly.yml` and `watchdog.yml`. It does nothing until the `HC_PING_URL` secret exists.
- **Supabase migrations** (new numbered files; `collect-*` jobs untouched):
  - `token-expiry` captures the `github-authentication-token-expiration` header from `net._http_response` into `collector_state` and `status_now`.
  - `site-freshness` fetches the site's `build.json` every 6 h into `status_now.site_data_through`. The live badge says when the data is more than 2 days stale.
- **Corrections log:** `docs/corrections.md` (date, what was wrong, before and after, version), shown on the Limits page.
- **CI:** `actions/attest-build-provenance` on Release uploads (free). `frictionless validate` of the data-branch CSVs.
- **Weekly Claude audit:** a claude.ai scheduled task on Mondays at a jittered minute, using the GitHub connector if the session holds it, working from `docs/audit-checklist.md`:
  - invariants and anomaly output;
  - 3 random trips traced from raw to site;
  - token expiry and database size trend;
  - islandtransit.org service changes;
  - the holiday list;
  - copy drift.
  It posts one comment to a pinned "Weekly audit" issue and opens pull requests only.
- **Accept:** a rehearsal with an edited `daily.csv` produces each tier. With no secret, the ping step logs "skipped".

## Phase 3: Clear, simple site (sessions 7–10)

**Data (new `pipeline/periods.py`, `periods/0.1.0`, a nightly step after quality):**
- `ontime/headline.json`: the rolling 28 **service days, spanning timetable changes**, so it does not reset on December 27. Holds counts, share, the previous 28 days, the change in points, and a sparkline series. The previous window stays null until it has 14 or more service days (about January 13, 2027); until then the site says "not enough days yet".
- `ontime/periods.json` with grains:
  - `hour`: hour of day, pooled over 28 days, plus the latest day;
  - `day`;
  - `week`: Monday-start, Pacific;
  - `month`: partial months flagged.
  Counts are summed. Medians come from pooled `deviations_s`.
- `quality/periods.json`, the same grains. Each metric declares how it pools: ratio of sums weighted by `scheduled_trips`, time-weighted, or a daily median with a range. A new `by_hour` block in the quality day files (`quality/0.6.0`) covers coverage, timepoint coverage, off-shape and position age. Metrics without hourly data say "not measured by hour".
- `scripts/site_data.py` `wanted()` copies them, and I8 reconciles them.

**Site:**
- `lib.js` `periodInput()`: a segmented radiogroup (Hour / Day / Week / Month, default Day) with arrow keys. It is shared through `?period=` and `localStorage`, and used as `viewof period` on `ontime.qmd` and `quality.qmd`.
- **Home:** the 28-day headline, the change, a sparkline (`charts.trend` `spark` option), the early / on-time / late split, a one-line trust status from `quality/notes.json` and `status_now`, and the "data through" date.
- `charts.figure` gets a `methods` option that adds a "How we measured this" link after `details.as-table` (`site/charts.js` L93). Add stable `{#id}` anchors to the `docs/methods.md` headings.
- New stacked early / on-time / late bars (`charts.stack`). Add the new colors to `MARKS` in `tests/test_site_tokens.py`.
- **Quality page:** seven tall charts become small multiples on one period axis. One rounding rule (`lib.fmtShare`) serves stats and titles alike.
- **Map:** `map.js` `highlightStop(id)` and a hover/click callback on `dotRange`, so the map and the chart highlight each other. Stop tooltips use `direction: "auto"`.
- **Fixes:**
  - inline SVG in place of `icon: github` (saves 176 KB per page);
  - no d3 or Plot on `data.qmd`;
  - "NAS Whidbey" names in `routeTitle`;
  - a shorter On time intro;
  - "Each link opens" wording.
- **New `site/limits.qmd`, "What this data can and can't tell you".** It covers sources, collection, timepoint-only scoring, missed first and last stops (depot, route changes, driver swaps), GPS glitches, schedule checks (the printed-time agreement figure) and the corrections log. Numbers are filled in from data files. Linked from the headline and the trust line.

## Phase 4: Fair comparisons (sessions 11–12)
- **Peers from NTD** (data.transportation.gov, keyless): bus, 15–60 vehicles in peak service, rural or small urban, with fare-free agencies marked. NTD has no on-time figures, so it is used for the peer group only.
- **Feeds from the Mobility Database catalog** (keyless CSV).
- **Three classes, each labeled on the page:**
  - **R, exact reverse:** Island Transit's own deviations recounted under each peer's published definition (`reference/peers/definitions.csv`: URL, window, measured moment, cancellations, all stops or timepoints).
  - **A, our own method:** a 2-hour sample of their GTFS-RT, once a week per agency at local peak or midday, run through `infer_day` on their static GTFS. Ranges come from a cluster bootstrap by trip, plus a stop-radius sensitivity run at 30, 40 and 50 m.
  - **B, converted:** their published share is converted to our window through a family of deviation distributions (class A agencies' and our own, shifted and scaled to reproduce their figure). Reported as a range, never a single number.
- **New `compare.yml`,** one cron per region. It keeps summaries only, about 3 KB per sample, in `compare/<agency>/<date>.json` and `compare/summary.json`. No raw data from other agencies is kept. It has its own version (`compare/0.1.0`) and invariants.
- **Candidates,** each checked against the catalog in session 11:
  - Washington: Skagit, Intercity (fare-free), Kitsap.
  - Other states: Missoula Mountain Line, Cache Valley.
  - Canada: BC Transit, Halifax.
  - Central and South America: Santiago, Mexico City or Bogotá (likely class B).
  - Europe: Isle of Wight (UK BODS, free key), Texel or Zeeland (OVapi).
  - Asia: Rapid Penang, Singapore (B), Israel (Open Bus Stride), Moscow (published figures only).
- **New `site/compare.qmd`:** a dot-and-range chart, class R first, each row saying how it was compared, with a plain note on why raw side-by-side figures mislead. Wording about other agencies stays factual and fair.

## Phase 5: Ambitious extras (sessions 13–15)
- **Stop view and trip view:**
  - New `scripts/site_views.py` runs in the Pages job. It downloads 28 days of stop-events parquet server-side (checked by SHA), so the CORS problem doesn't apply.
  - It writes `views/stops/<stop>.json` (about 15 KB each, about 2.5 MB total), kept in the site artifact only, so the data branch doesn't grow.
  - Pick a stop to see its recent departures, scheduled against actual, with a deviation dot plot. A trip view shows the plan against inferred times, with `report_source`.
  - A light invariant checks it against `by_route_stop`.
- **Replay a day:** `views/replay/<date>.json` for the last 14 days, with quantized, delta-coded positions (about 400 KB gzipped), loaded on demand, with a Leaflet time slider and buses colored by minutes late. Route changes are visible.
- **Official validators:**
  - MobilityData gtfs-validator (pinned jar) on each new Passio version, writing `validation/static/<version>.json`.
  - gtfs-realtime-validator in batch mode on one archived hour a week.
  - Both feed the Tier 2 rules.
- **Statistics:** a bootstrap interval on the headline (`ontime/0.5.0`); a quantile dot plot for prediction error ("of 20 buses predicted 5 minutes away…"); the gap between reports stored on each event.

## Risks and limits
- Each version bump rebuilds every day, 7 per run, so raise `--max-days` for one-off catch-ups.
- Borrowing reports raises exposure to glitches, which is why the glitch filter ships first.
- Comparison feeds vanish and keys expire; treat a missing sample as normal.
- Free-plan limits: GitHub Pages 1 GB; keep the data branch small, so views stay in the artifact; Supabase 500 MB and 5 GB egress.
- Testing comparison feeds from the sandbox needs new allowed hosts, which shows Davin an approval prompt.
